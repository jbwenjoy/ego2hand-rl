# Capacity response supervision protocol

After the first small A1/A2 Actor comparison, test whether response labels improve the direction of physical action suggestions. No policy or actuator model is updated in this comparison. The two capacity fits use the same ordinary sequences and TRAIN basis endpoints, common initialization, matched minibatches and optimizer work. The additional loss uses finite capacity contrasts from actual native actions at the same fully restored state. Distinguish action clipping, joint-limit saturation, and changes no larger than the repeated sham difference. Positive and negative basis directions cover the eight actions for fingers 2 and 3. Mixed directions and the third behavior's labels are excluded from optimization.

Each native capacity label is the mean of eight 72-direction supports with a common 1 N normal budget in the moving BODY frame at each tick. Each exact-source branch retains its raw contact trace, delivered PD, response packet and the complete source snapshot. Report both one-step absolute prediction and directional change, including sign, amplitude and RMSE relative to predicting no change. Distinguish capacity-vector error from the scalar deviation from the reference capacity, and identify cases where the contact geom-pair multiset changes. The independent saved packet comparison on previously inspected policies uses directions originally chosen to reduce shaft angle.

For the model-chosen action test use the scripted, preassigned ten source phases and 0.02 RAW L2 radius. The target is active in eight intervals. Both heads propose a direction before executing any branches at that source. Each proposal, its opposite and the shared clipped base run from the same W1 snapshot twice with real PD and physics checks. The new residual A1_7413 Actor is withheld from both capacity-head fits. Its ancestry and prior native evaluations are known. The final support policy is an additional previously inspected cohort. The strict win count requires both descent repeats to beat both baseline and opposite repeats by more than the baseline spread. No stage tuning, post-hoc sign reversal, policy update or full-reference success claim follows from this test.
