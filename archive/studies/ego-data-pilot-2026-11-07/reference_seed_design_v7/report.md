Required upward force passes all three enumerated supporting halfspaces of the original force-ray cone with positive margins (.661–.928 N); this check therefore supplies no force-direction exclusion. It does not allocate forces or establish wrench equilibrium.

The torque geometry is much more restrictive. The index contacts are only6.494 µm apart. About the line from the thumb point to the index centroid, gravity contributes .00741812 Nm. For native four-ray μ=.7 forces, `|F|≤sqrt(1+μ²)Fn`; consequently the maximum contact moment about that line with each finger's total Fn≤3 N is only1.18515e−5 Nm, 626 times smaller. The thumb point has zero lever about that line. Without the normal cap, compensating this axial moment alone needs at least1877.8 N in the most optimistic lever bound; that is not a realizable force proposal, and the uncapped full-wrench LP still had positive residual. These are local statements about the unchanged saved points, not global infeasibility.

Two translations illustrate why the geometry must change. The shortest COM displacement that removes only this line moment, **holding world contact points fixed**, is6.74 mm and mostly phone −Y (`[-.333,-6.686,+.762] mm` in body coordinates). Such a displacement is comparable to the phone's thickness and does not preserve contact; it is not a valid pose recommendation. Moving only along body +Z would need59.55 mm to eliminate that one moment. A more directly interpretable seed moves the pinch material region from the phone's upper end toward its COM: the finger-balanced contact height is49.390 mm, giving a half/full longitudinal slide of24.695/49.390 mm. Body +Z is palm `[-.7723,+.6078,-.1848]`.
